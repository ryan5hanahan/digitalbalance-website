The philosophy

Meeting you where you are.

We're not going to tell you to delete Instagram. You've probably already tried that.

Digital Balance works like a strength-training program — not a diet. You're not cutting things out; you're building the capacity to choose. The skills stay with you after the program ends.

The goal isn't zero screen time. The goal is intentional screen time — knowing why you picked up the phone, and being okay with whatever that answer is.

"The question isn't whether you use technology. It's whether you're using it — or it's using you."

Digital Balance doesn't shame you for being online. It builds the psychological flexibility to act in accordance with your own values — not the ones the algorithm decided for you.

Why now

The law is catching up to what you already feel.

Juries are starting to agree. In 2026 a Los Angeles jury found Meta and YouTube negligent in the first trial over a young person's compulsive social media use, and a New Mexico jury found Meta misled the public about child safety.

In August, Meta agreed to pay up to $17 billion and change its products for teens to settle a case brought by state attorneys general — without admitting wrongdoing. European regulators have preliminarily found that TikTok's and Meta's addictive design breaks EU law, and Australia now keeps under-16s off social media.

Digital Balance exists because people deserve tools to help themselves — right now, before the law finishes catching up.

As of October 2026. Several rulings are under appeal.

K.G.M. v. Meta Platforms, Inc., et al.

The first jury verdict in the youth social media addiction cases: Meta and YouTube found negligent, $6 million awarded. Snap and TikTok settled before trial.

Los Angeles Superior Court, JCCP No. 5255 · March 2026

People of the State of California et al. v. Meta Platforms, Inc.

Settled during trial for up to about $17 billion, with required teen safeguards including default time limits and nighttime blocks. No admission of wrongdoing.

N.D. Cal. No. 4:23-cv-05448 · August 2026

European Commission, Digital Services Act

Preliminary findings that TikTok's and Meta's infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications and recommender systems are addictive design that breaches EU law. Not yet final.

How it works

Three steps. Your pace.

1

Take a short agency assessment

A few questions about your values, habits, and relationship with technology — not a screen time quiz. The program calibrates to where you actually are.

2

Each lesson takes a few minutes. Real exercises follow real content. Daily check-ins, commitment practices, values clarification. Actual work, not passive reading.
